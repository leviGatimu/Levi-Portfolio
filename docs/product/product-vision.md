# Product Vision

## One paragraph

A personal portfolio for **Levi Gatimu** — Year 2 student developer at NGA Coding Academy, Kigali — that works as **proof of work**. It presents a small number of real projects (full-stack web, desktop, AI, simulation, robotics) as large, art-directed case studies on an editorial dark canvas driven by typography and imagery rather than UI chrome. Behind it sits a private CMS so a new project can be published without touching code.

## The thought sequence we are designing for

1. *"This is a developer."* — the masthead and opening statement establish identity in two seconds.
2. *"He actually builds real projects."* — Selected Work leads with large real screenshots and concrete one-liners.
3. *"He works across full-stack, AI, robotics and systems."* — the range is shown by the projects themselves, not by a skills cloud.
4. *"I want to see how he built these."* — case studies explain problem, approach, architecture, decisions and lessons.

Every page and section must serve at least one step of this sequence. If it does not, it is cut.

## Positioning statement

> A highly designed personal engineering workspace — editorial on the surface, technically deep underneath.

- **Editorial** — composition, large type, whitespace, thin rules, small metadata, asymmetry.
- **Engineering workspace** — the content reads like someone's real notebook of built things: architecture, decisions, trade-offs, what broke.
- **Personal** — one real person, one real portrait, honest scale (a Year 2 student who ships), aviation as a quiet through-line, not a gimmick.

## Principles (ranked)

1. **Truth over impression.** Nothing invented. Modest real numbers beat impressive fake ones. A student who ships is more compelling than a "10x developer" template.
2. **Work first.** Projects get the largest visual budget on every page. The name and portrait establish identity, then get out of the way.
3. **Typography does the heavy lifting.** If a section looks empty without decoration, the type and spacing are wrong — fix those, don't add decoration.
4. **Composed, not assembled.** Sections are designed as compositions on a grid, not stacked components. No card grids as the default answer.
5. **Motion is seasoning.** Subtle, purposeful, fully optional. The site must be excellent with motion off.
6. **Maintainable by one student.** Small stack, few dependencies, a CMS a tired person can use at midnight.

## Success criteria for V1

- A visitor can, without scrolling past the fold, tell who Levi is, where he is, and what he does.
- The featured projects are visible with real screenshots within the first two scroll lengths on desktop.
- Every published project has a case study with at least: overview, role, stack, one real image, and one link (repo or live).
- Levi can add and publish a new project end-to-end in under 15 minutes without opening a code editor.
- Lighthouse (mobile): Performance ≥ 90, Accessibility 100, Best Practices ≥ 95, SEO 100 on `/`, `/work`, and one `/work/[slug]`.
- No text on the site is untrue or unverified.
